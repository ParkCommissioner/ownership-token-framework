# SKY / MKR: Automated buybacks through the migration

This page does not have a standalone long-form report in `files.zip`, so the SKY write-up is assembled from the week-two research brief and the original chart prompt. The reason it still belongs here is that the MKR to SKY migration is one of the most important normalization problems in the dataset: price, market cap, and supply history cross a `1:24,000` unit change on **September 18, 2024**, but the underlying surplus-buyback thesis remains continuous across that line.

## Why SKY matters in the comparison

MakerDAO, and later Sky, is treated throughout the research as the clearest benchmark for **automated, non-discretionary buybacks**. The core claim is simple: protocol surplus should recycle into the token without reopening the question in governance every quarter. That makes SKY the strongest contrast case against more discretionary programs like AAVE's Buy and Distribute or Jupiter's debated buyback budget.

## Timeline used for the chart

- **January 26, 2022:** Rune Christensen publishes the Endgame Plan, framing buybacks and SubDAO structure as the intended destination.
- **September 1, 2023:** The Smart Burn Engine activates and begins automatically buying MKR from protocol surplus.
- **September 18, 2024:** MKR rebrands to SKY at `1:24,000`; this is the critical normalization breakpoint for price and supply data.
- **February 1, 2025:** Buyback scale becomes a visible part of the narrative as spending approaches roughly `$1M/day`.
- **March 1, 2025:** The LP-heavy design is abandoned in favor of a simpler direct-buyback path.

## What the research emphasizes

- **Automation is the differentiator.** SKY's key advantage is not just that buybacks happen, but that they are perceived as structurally entrenched rather than discretionary.
- **The migration cannot be charted naively.** Pre-migration MKR history has to be converted into SKY-equivalent units or the price and circulating-supply series will look broken.
- **Simplicity beats tokenomic plumbing.** The shift away from the LP-heavy structure fits a broader theme across the dataset: simpler value-accrual paths remain legible for markets and governance much longer than complex ones.

## Working conclusion

SKY is not presented as a perfect success story. The rebrand created execution and communication challenges, and staking only became part of the story after the original burn era. But in the comparative research it remains the strongest example of an **automated buyback signal that retained credibility through change**, which is why it sits near the top of the benchmark set for this project.
