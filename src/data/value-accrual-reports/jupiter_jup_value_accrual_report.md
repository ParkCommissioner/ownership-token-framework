# Jupiter (JUP): When $70M in Buybacks Doesn't Move the Price

**Prepared for: CoW DAO Value Distribution Engagement — Comparative Protocol Case Study**

---

## Why Jupiter matters for this analysis

Jupiter is the single most important counter-evidence to the Hyperliquid buyback thesis. The protocol spent **$70 million on token buybacks in 2025** and the token still fell **88%** from its peak. Jupiter's CTO himself called it a **"waste of resources"** ([AMBCrypto](https://ambcrypto.com/waste-of-resources-jupiter-cto-explains-why-jup-buybacks-may-end)). For CoW DAO, this is the data point that prevents uncritical adoption of the "just buy back more" framework. It proves that buybacks fail when token emissions overwhelm them — and forces the engagement to confront CoW's own emission schedule as the binding constraint on any mechanism design.

---

## Protocol overview

Jupiter is Solana's dominant DEX aggregator, processing the majority of Solana swap volume and expanding into perpetuals, DCA tools, a launchpad (LFG), a stablecoin (JupUSD backed 90% by BlackRock's BUIDL), and cross-chain infrastructure (JupNet) ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/price/jupiter); [OKX](https://www.okx.com/en-us/learn/jupiter-burn-staking-defi)). Cumulative protocol revenue reached **$369 million** by late 2025 ([AMBCrypto](https://ambcrypto.com/waste-of-resources-jupiter-cto-explains-why-jup-buybacks-may-end)). It is the most commercially successful aggregator by revenue, making its token's underperformance especially instructive.

## Token distribution and the supply problem

JUP launched in January 2024 with an initial total supply of **10 billion tokens**. In January 2025, governance approved a 30% supply reduction and burn, bringing total supply to approximately **7 billion** ([Gate.io](https://www.gate.com/learn/articles/jupiter-jup-tokenomics-governance-utility-explained)). The allocation split:

- **50% to community** (airdrops, ASR rewards, ecosystem)
- **50% to team/insiders** (2 billion reserved for future team members, investors, stakeholders with minimum 1-year lock-up) ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/price/jupiter))

The critical issue is the **emissions schedule**. Monthly unlocks of approximately **53 million JUP** continue through June 2026, adding ~$11.7M in sell pressure monthly at current prices ([CoinMarketCap](https://coinmarketcap.com/cmc-ai/jupiter-ag/price-prediction)). Since launch, circulating supply has increased by roughly **150%** ([Yellow](https://yellow.com/news/jupiter-founder-questions-dollar70-million-buyback-strategy-after-89-price-decline); [Crypto.news](https://crypto.news/jupiter-jup-token-buyback-unlocks-solana-2026)). The "Jupuary" airdrops further expanded supply: 1 billion tokens in January 2024, 700 million planned for January 2025, later reduced to 200 million for January 2026 after community pushback ([CoinMarketCap](https://coinmarketcap.com/cmc-ai/jupiter-ag/price-prediction)).

---

## The buyback program: $70M spent, 88% price decline

In January 2025, alongside the supply reduction vote, Jupiter activated a buyback mechanism: **50% of protocol fee revenue** is used to repurchase JUP tokens ([Gate.io](https://www.gate.com/learn/articles/jupiter-jup-tokenomics-governance-utility-explained); [OKX](https://www.okx.com/en-us/learn/jupiter-burn-staking-defi)). Repurchased tokens are split between immediate burns and a **"long-term litterbox"** — a reserve locked for future strategic use ([OKX](https://www.okx.com/en-us/learn/jupiter-burn-staking-defi)).

The results were devastating for the buyback thesis:

- Jupiter spent approximately **$57.9–70 million** on buybacks throughout 2025 ([CoinMarketCap](https://coinmarketcap.com/cmc-ai/jupiter-ag/price-prediction); [AMBCrypto](https://ambcrypto.com/waste-of-resources-jupiter-cto-explains-why-jup-buybacks-may-end))
- The buybacks covered only about **6% of unlocked tokens** ([Yellow](https://yellow.com/news/jupiter-founder-questions-dollar70-million-buyback-strategy-after-89-price-decline))
- The token fell from its peak of **$1.80 to $0.20** — an **88% crash** — printing new yearly lows *despite* the aggressive buyback program ([AMBCrypto](https://ambcrypto.com/waste-of-resources-jupiter-cto-explains-why-jup-buybacks-may-end))
- JUP did rally approximately **300% in the first month** after the buyback launched in mid-February 2025, but this initial pump quickly reversed as ongoing unlocks overwhelmed the buyback bid ([AMBCrypto](https://ambcrypto.com/waste-of-resources-jupiter-cto-explains-why-jup-buybacks-may-end))
- JUP **underperformed SOL by 38% over 90 days** despite similar ecosystem activity ([CoinMarketCap](https://coinmarketcap.com/cmc-ai/jupiter-ag/price-prediction))

As of March 2026, JUP trades at approximately **$0.15** with a market cap of roughly **$595M** ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/price/jupiter)).

## The buyback debate: CTO calls it "waste of resources"

On January 3, 2026, Jupiter co-founder and CTO **Siong Ong** posted publicly: **"We spent more than $70m on buyback last year, and the price obviously didn't move much. We can use the $70m to give out for growth incentives for existing and new users. Should we do it?"** ([AMBCrypto](https://ambcrypto.com/waste-of-resources-jupiter-cto-explains-why-jup-buybacks-may-end); [Crypto.news](https://crypto.news/jupiter-jup-token-buyback-unlocks-solana-2026)).

He followed Helium founder Amir Haleem, who announced days earlier: "the market doesn't seem to care about projects buying their tokens back off the market, so we are going to stop wasting our money" ([BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/jupiter-buyback-solana-token-unlocks)).

Solana co-founder **Anatoly Yakovenko** proposed an alternative model: **"Protocols should actually stash the cash for a future buyback. This would force all the unlocks to trade at the future expected post buyback price. Let people lockup and stake for a year to get a token yield. So as the balance sheet grows those who stake net a bigger claim"** ([BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/jupiter-buyback-solana-token-unlocks); [Yellow](https://yellow.com/news/jupiter-founder-questions-dollar70-million-buyback-strategy-after-89-price-decline)). His model emphasizes long-term capital formation over short-term buybacks, aiming to extend the utility cycle of funds and strengthen token value anchoring.

Ong recognized that with 53 million JUP unlocking monthly through June 2026, "buybacks serve only as a short-term buffer rather than a sustainable long-term support mechanism" and argued it would be "inefficient to continue allocating capital to buybacks" ([Bitget](https://www.bitget.com/amp/news/detail/12560605130545); [Crypto.news](https://crypto.news/jupiter-jup-token-buyback-unlocks-solana-2026)).

## Forum debates: the community is split

The Jupiter governance forum ([discuss.jup.ag](https://discuss.jup.ag)) reveals deep divisions on what to do next:

### Pro-staking revenue sharing

One forum user calculated: "With 753 million JUP staked, that's almost $0.09 per JUP. For me personally, that would be a really nice passive income. That's a 43% dividend yield. Of course the price would pump on such news" ([AMBCrypto](https://ambcrypto.com/waste-of-resources-jupiter-cto-explains-why-jup-buybacks-may-end)).

The "[A Plan to Improve $JUP Value](https://discuss.jup.ag/t/a-plan-to-improve-jup-value/39718)" thread (November 2025) proposed: continue buybacks but redirect them entirely to Active Staking Rewards (ASR), creating "zero emission ASR" and a "true revenue sharing model." The post also suggested reducing the 2026 Jupuary from 700M to 500M and burning the difference, re-examining the unstaking period (currently 7 days), and considering distributing ASR in other Jupiter assets (JLP, JupUSD, JupSOL) if JUP rewards created unnecessary sell pressure.

### Pro-growth (anti-buyback)

CTO Ong questioned how the product would grow if everything were allocated to staking rewards ([AMBCrypto](https://ambcrypto.com/waste-of-resources-jupiter-cto-explains-why-jup-buybacks-may-end)). The team ultimately cut the 2026 Jupuary airdrop from 700M to 200M JUP and is considering redirecting 50% of protocol fees to staker rewards instead of buybacks, which could produce an estimated **25% APY** ([CoinMarketCap](https://coinmarketcap.com/cmc-ai/jupiter-ag/price-prediction); [Bitget](https://www.bitget.com/amp/news/detail/12560605130545)).

### Pro-burn

The "[Proposal to Burn the Litterbox](https://discuss.jup.ag/t/new-dao-vote-proposal-to-burn-the-litterbox/39627)" thread (November 2025) debated burning the ~130M JUP accumulated in the litterbox (~$60M+ value). One dissenter argued: "Burning feels decisive, but it's really just destroying $60M+ in real capital for a fleeting price bump that'll fade in days. Instead of burning, one option could be to redirect all 130M into an ASR booster pool over the next 12–24 months, giving every staker a solid 4–6% yield boost" ([discuss.jup.ag](https://discuss.jup.ag/t/new-dao-vote-proposal-to-burn-the-litterbox/39627/16)).

### The equity problem

Analyst Fabiano stated that **"there is currently no reason to hold the token because it is not tied to the protocol's success (not equity)"** ([AMBCrypto](https://ambcrypto.com/waste-of-resources-jupiter-cto-explains-why-jup-buybacks-may-end)). A short-term solution, he suggested, would be sharing revenue with stakers to reduce their quarterly dumping pressure. This echoes the core challenge for all aggregator governance tokens — without a revenue claim, the token is a governance receipt with no economic floor.

### Governance decay

A January 2026 forum post titled "[Guaranteed Staking Rewards](https://discuss.jup.ag/t/guaranteed-staking-rewards/39861)" documented declining governance activity: **"Q1 2025 - 5 proposals, Q2 2025 - 3, Q3 2025 - 1, Q4 2025 - 0, Q1 2026 - 0. This means that there is no incentive to stake JUP."** The poster proposed guaranteeing quarterly rewards regardless of whether proposals exist to vote on.

## Active Staking Rewards (ASR): the mechanics

Jupiter's staking program distributes rewards quarterly to users who stake JUP and participate in governance voting. Rewards are proportional to staked amount and voting activity ([Gate.io](https://www.gate.com/learn/articles/jupiter-jup-tokenomics-governance-utility-explained); [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/price/jupiter)). Funding comes from protocol revenue (LFG Launchpad fees, trading fees) and a dedicated JUP incentive pool. The unstaking process has a **30-day delay** ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/price/jupiter)).

In practice, ASR distributed 50M JUP in Q1 2025's claim period ([discuss.jup.ag](https://discuss.jup.ag/t/asr-active-staking-rewards-methodology/19338/75)). But with governance activity declining to zero proposals by Q4 2025, the "active" part of Active Staking Rewards became vestigial — there is nothing to actively participate in.

## Why Jupiter failed where Hyperliquid succeeded

The comparison is instructive because both ran large-scale buyback programs in 2025:

| Dimension | Hyperliquid | Jupiter |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Buyback spending (2025)** | ~$644M | ~$70M |
| **VC/team unlock pressure** | Zero VC; team cliff Nov 2025 | 53M JUP/month continuous |
| **Buyback as % of new supply** | Net positive (buybacks > unlocks for most of 2025) | 6% of unlocked tokens |
| **Price performance** | +950% from TGE | -88% from peak |
| **Revenue trajectory** | Growing (record months in 2025) | Declining from peak |

The difference is not mechanism design — both used revenue-funded buybacks. The difference is **net flow dynamics**. Hyperliquid's buybacks exceeded team unlocks for most of 2025 because there were no VC tokens and the cliff hadn't hit yet. Jupiter's buybacks were a garden hose against a firehose of supply: 150% increase in circulating supply overwhelmed $70M in repurchases.

Yakovenko's diagnosis captures it precisely: "In markets with heavy emissions, short-term buybacks do not reset how sellers price risk. Tokens unlocked today are sold at today's price, not at some future value implied by ongoing repurchases" ([Crypto.news](https://crypto.news/jupiter-jup-token-buyback-unlocks-solana-2026)).

## Key lessons for CoW DAO

**1. Buybacks cannot overcome emissions.** Jupiter spent $70M buying back tokens while $1.2B in unlocks hit the market. The buybacks covered 6% of new supply. CoW must model its own emission/unlock schedule against projected buyback capacity before committing to any mechanism.

**2. The 300% initial pump is a trap.** JUP rallied 300% in the first month after announcing buybacks, then gave it all back and more. The announcement effect is real but transient. CoW should not mistake initial market enthusiasm for sustained mechanism effectiveness.

**3. The Yakovenko model deserves serious consideration.** Accumulating revenue for future deployment, combined with time-locked staking that gives holders a forward claim on accumulated value, addresses the timing mismatch that killed Jupiter's program. This is conceptually similar to Sky's Staking Engine but with an explicit deferred-buyback commitment.

**4. Governance activity decay kills staking incentives.** When proposals drop to zero, governance-gated staking rewards become pointless. CoW should design staking rewards that are either unconditional (tied to revenue, not governance activity) or ensure consistent governance throughput.

**5. The CTO's honesty matters.** Ong's "waste of resources" statement is the most candid assessment of buyback effectiveness from any protocol founder. If buybacks can't outpace emissions at $70M/year, they certainly can't at CoW's $14–19M/year. The mechanism design must look beyond buybacks alone.

**6. The "not equity" problem is the root cause.** Fabiano's critique — that JUP has no reason to be held because it's not tied to protocol success — applies to every aggregator governance token including COW. Revenue sharing (either via direct distribution or via buyback-and-distribute to stakers) is the only path to creating an economic floor beneath a governance token.
